What “control” means here, and how it’s calculated

Flagged group (10): pitchers pulled from error_analysis_v2.md’s worked examples — three (Suárez, Montas, Rodriguez) appear in both the worst-over- and worst-under-prediction lists (alternating between huge misses in opposite directions), plus repeat over-predictions with a known real-world workload-management storyline (Skenes, Crochet, Yamamoto, Kershaw, J. Ryan, J. Steele) and one repeat-under-prediction case in the opposite direction (Gausman, who just goes deep relentlessly).

Control group (15): a fair “what does normal look like” baseline — every 2024 val-season pitcher with 15+ starts (the same established-starter bar the flagged group clears, so it's apples-to-apples, not comparing a veteran against a part-timer), excluding the flagged names, then a random sample of 15 from what's left (fixed seed, not cherry-picked).

Metric: for every pitcher in both groups, the season-long coefficient of variation (std ÷ mean) of realized_batters_faced per start. CV instead of raw std because it normalizes for a pitcher's own average workload — a 26-BF/start ace and an 18-BF/start innings-eater shouldn't be compared on raw swing size.

Trailing-N-start volatility needs new function

Std dev (or CV) of realized_batters_faced over the pitcher's last 3–5 starts, mirroring the existing pitcher_last3_start_pa_* trailing-window pattern already in game_context.py. Distinct from everything v2 already has: season average (level), 3-start trend (direction), and workload density — none of those capture that a pitcher is unpredictable independent of his recent average or trend.

Caveat to test, not assume: this measures uncertainty, not direction — it tells the model “this one's a coin flip” but not which way the next start will land. It may help XGBoost's splits (e.g. discount the cascade harder when volatility is high) more than it moves aggregate MAE on its own — worth checking both in a real v4 run.
