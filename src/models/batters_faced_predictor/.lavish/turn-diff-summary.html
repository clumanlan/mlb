What this turn actually established

1

Re-ran the residual diagnostic against v2's model, not v1's gap closed

The only prior residual analysis (experiments/residual_error_analysis/) predates v2/v3 and was never re-run against the current strongest model. Sanity-checked MAE 2.6471 matches v2_results.md exactly. Bucket cuts (weather, month, day-of-week, doubleheader, venue, score margin, opposing-lineup strength, rest days, workload density, trend direction) all came back flat — no hidden lever there.

2

Worked examples surfaced a persistent failure mode still open

Established, high-confidence starters (cascade weight 0.7–0.86) pulled after 0–2 clean innings, 23/25 with no decision recorded — the same over-prediction mode v2 targeted but didn't close (established-bucket MAE only moved 2.6053→2.5154). Three names (Suárez, Montas, Rodriguez) appeared in both the worst-over and worst-under lists.

3

Volatility hypothesis confirmed real, then self-corrected under scrutiny honest correction

Full 2024 game logs for the flagged pitchers vs. a random 15-pitcher control group confirmed elevated CV (0.238 vs. 0.139, 9/10 flagged pitchers above every control pitcher). But a follow-up breakdown (prompted by direct pushback) showed CV is driven by just 1–3 anomalous starts per pitcher (42–96% of variance) across all ten flagged pitchers — not chronic game-to-game unpredictability as first framed. A trailing-3–5-start rolling std-dev (the original v4 candidate) can't see these coming; the original proposal didn't survive its own evidence.

4

Rest-days hypothesis tested directly and ruled out falsified cleanly

User's hypothesis (insufficient rest before the short outing) checked against pitcher_days_since_last_start for the 9 clearest events: 6, 6, 5, 6, 7, 6, 8, 5, 6 days — normal-to-extra rest, nothing short. Weather also ruled out (no rain/delays). Day-of-week/month showed only a thin, inconclusive skew (n=19, too small to act on).

Open decision

No pre-game-knowable shared trigger survived across rest days, weather, day-of-week, opponent, or workload density. The remaining plausible mechanisms (in-game score state at the moment of the pull, external injury/roster-management news) are either not pre-game-knowable or not present in this pipeline's data. Leaning candidate: a season-to-date count of a pitcher's own anomalously-short starts — a base-rate/recency signal rather than a rolling-volatility one — not yet built, pending direction on whether to scope it as a v4 experiment or investigate the in-game score-state angle first (which, even if it explains the mechanism, can't become a usable feature since it isn't knowable pre-game).
