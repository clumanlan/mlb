Update: chasing the low-PA tail

2026-08-23

User's instinct: focus on batters who get ≤3 PA (early-exit / compressed-game side of the tail), reasoning it's more predictable — a "strong pitcher vs. weak batter" effect. Pulled real 2024 data to test it before writing any model code.

Batting order alone is the dominant mechanical driver — mostly expected. The real question: controlling for order ≥ 6, is there a second, independent signal?

Correlation with team's total PA that gamerReads as
Opposing starter's WHIP (that game) +0.47 strong
Opposing starter's pitches/inning +0.40 strong
Opposing starter's innings pitched −0.42 confound — length follows from dominance
Opposing starter's K-rate −0.004 ~none
The mechanism isn't strikeouts — it's traffic. A pitcher who avoids walks/hits compresses the whole game's PA count regardless of how many batters he strikes out, which is what pushes a bottom-of-order hitter below their 4th trip. Controlling for batting order ≥ 6: WHIP 1.03 (low-tail games) vs. 1.64 (normal games) — a large, real gap, not noise.

Decision: interaction, not a filtered submodel

Considered restricting training data to batting_order ≥ 6 (a specialist low-tail classifier) vs. adding WHIP as a feature on the full dataset and letting a tree model find the order × WHIP interaction itself. Chose the latter first — cheaper to test, keeps one general model instead of two, and XGBoost already had batting_order in v1; it just never had WHIP to interact it with.

This matches the earlier read: the WHIP effect is real but lives in a narrow slice (order ≥ 6) and shifts probability mass at the margin (3 vs 4 PA) — a shift that regression MAE on the full distribution is the wrong lens to see.

Next: reframe as a binary classifier, P(n_pa ≤ 3), scored with log loss/Brier/ROC-AUC instead of MAE — not a filtered submodel, but evaluated on the slice where the signal actually lives.

Structural ceiling on the naive floor: the per-slot lookup can never predict above ~59% confidence (the 9-hole's own historical rate) — no matter how the threshold is set. XGBoost @ 0.85 — 64.7% precision, 95% CI [55.6%, 72.8%], n=116 val predictions (~1 qualifying game every 2 days across a season). Tighter CI and higher point estimate than 0.8, and it beats LR's overall PR-AUC edge specifically at this selective, low-volume end — matches the stated use case directly ("not going to be making many bets, selective anyway"). LR's own n=24 at 0.85 is too thin to trust.
