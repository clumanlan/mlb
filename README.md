# MLB Prop Research

An ML system for MLB prop research (DraftKings), built around a specific bet: **the way to find real signal isn't to model outcomes better, it's to pick outcomes that have less irreducible noise in them.**

---

## The thesis

The first model here (`hit_predictor`) threw 100+ engineered features and XGBoost at "will this batter get a hit in this plate appearance," aggregated up to the game grain a DraftKings prop actually resolves on. Five model versions in, it still hasn't beaten a naive baseline. Not because the features are wrong — because **a single plate appearance's outcome is mostly noise no feature set can remove.**

Once contact is made, what happens next — exit velocity, launch angle, exactly where nine fielders happen to be standing, whether a liner finds a glove or a gap — is close to a coin flip even for the best hitters in the league (this is why BABIP is famous in sabermetrics: it barely varies by skill). A "run scored" is worse: it's several of those noisy events chained together across an inning, so the uncertainty compounds instead of averaging out.

So the project's direction shifted: instead of trying to out-model that noise, **decompose the prediction problem into sub-problems where the outcome is more dependent on data we have before the game starts** — a roster decision, a workload constraint, or a skill matchup that doesn't depend on where a ball happens to land. Model those instead. `BENCHMARKS.md` and `ROADMAP.md` have the full experimental history; this doc is the "why," kept short.

---

## The dividing line: decided-before-the-game vs. resolved-by-contact

Every candidate prediction target here gets sorted along one axis: **how much of its variance is settled by a legible, pre-game mechanism, versus by the physics of a ball in play (plus defense, park, luck) once contact happens?**

| | Resolved by contact / compounding luck | Resolved by pre-game mechanism / skill matchup |
|---|---|---|
| **What decides it** | Exit velo, launch angle, fielder positioning, park geometry — none of it knowable pre-game, most of it not attributable to either player's skill | A roster decision already made (batting order, bullpen usage) or a repeatable skill duel that never requires a ball in play (strikeout, walk) |
| **Noise floor** | High — BABIP-driven outcomes are close to random even for skilled hitters; a run adds *multiple* such draws in sequence | Low — the outcome doesn't route through contact/defense/luck at all, or is close to reading off a decision a team already made |
| **Sample stabilization** | Batting average needs ~800 PA to stabilize | Walk rate stabilizes ~120 PA, strikeout rate ~60 PA (no batted-ball luck in the numerator) |
| **Examples tried/considered** | Hit/no-hit per PA (`hit_predictor` v1–v5), run scored | `low_pa` (built), K-prop (built), BB-prop (built), starter early exit, bullpen day |

That's the whole reframe: **hit-per-PA and runs-scored sit on the noisy side of this line; everything in the current sub-problem menu sits on the mechanism side.**

**Why so many of these are pitcher-side targets:** it's not a coincidence — predicting a pitcher's outcome is itself a way of controlling for a single batter's plate-appearance variance. A starting pitcher faces far more batters in a game than any one batter gets plate appearances (~20+ vs. ~4), so a pitcher's game-level line is effectively averaged over many more "PA-equivalent" events. Same noise-reduction logic as the dividing line above, applied to *who* you pick as the unit of prediction, not just *what* you predict.

## Sub-problem menu

| Target | Mechanism | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **`low_pa`** — batter gets ≤3 plate appearances in a game | Low lineup slot + a starter who typically goes deep + pinch-hit risk — a batting-order decision interacting with a pitcher's known workload pattern | **Built, positive.** XGBoost @ 0.85 confidence → 64.7% precision (95% CI [55.6%, 72.8%]), ~116 qualifying predictions/season. Not yet wired into `hit_predictor`; de-vig check against real DK lines still required. |
| **Strikeout probability (K-prop)** | Pitcher stuff/command vs. batter contact skill — resolved without a ball ever being put in play | **Built, positive — now with a tuned production candidate.** Baseline LR beats both naive floors (PR-AUC 0.270 vs. 0.226, ROC-AUC 0.582 vs. 0.506); v1 pitcher-workload rolling features flat vs. baseline. v2 (opposing-batter/team K-rate, weather, times-through-order) raised PA-grain PR-AUC to 0.282 and deepened the game-grain verdict to `real_improvement` (resolution 0.013 vs. naive's 0.005) — beats naive on both reliability and resolution, the first such verdict anywhere in this project, and the strongest model in the repo at the time. An ad-hoc residual dive on the worst 2024 total-K misses (19 of 20 were under-predictions) then drove three follow-up passes: v3 (trailing-3-start form, opposing-team K-rate volatility, whiff rate/pitch efficiency — flat aggregate, but season-level whiff rate, the first pitch-level Statcast feature used anywhere in this project, ranked #7 of 42 features), v4 (fastball-only velocity/spin decline, isolated from pitch-mix composition since pooling every pitch type together was diluting the signal — still flat, but its direction-trend feature ranked #3 of 64, the strongest individual feature lead found anywhere in this project), and v5 (stripped down to just the features that mattered in v4 — still flat, closing the thread; v3/v4/v5 are statistically indistinguishable, so v3's leaner 42-feature set was carried forward). **v6 then properly tuned both LR and XGBoost for the first time** (every prior version used untuned defaults) — LR barely moved, but tuned XGBoost jumped from 0.274 to 0.284, beating LR outright and posting the best calibration + discrimination combination of any k_predictor version to date. Tuned XGBoost (shallow trees, low learning rate, early stopping) is now the standing production candidate — the first time a tree has beaten LR anywhere in this project, after every prior comparison only ever tested XGBoost at defaults. Scoped to starter-only PAs (a bullpen reliever isn't identifiable pre-game). Full numbers: `ROADMAP.md`'s k_predictor entries. |
| **Walk probability (BB-prop)** | Pitcher control/zone% vs. batter chase rate — same "three true outcomes" logic as K | **Built, mixed.** Baseline LR beats both naive floors (PR-AUC 0.096 vs. 0.076, ROC-AUC 0.577 vs. 0.501). Scoped to starting pitchers and starting batters only (same population-scoping as K-prop). v1 (rolling walk rate + times_through_order) essentially flat vs. baseline at PA grain (PR-AUC 0.100), though batter rolling walk rate is the single most important feature by a wide margin. Game-grain check ("1+ walk") came back `overconfidence_risk`, not `real_improvement` like K-prop's — real added discrimination (ROC-AUC 0.608 vs. naive's 0.557) but slightly worse calibration than the naive floor. Not yet experiment-ready. |
| **Short outing / bullpen day (≤4 IP or an explicit opener)** | Recent workload/pitch-count trend, opponent's platoon-advantage depth, bullpen rest state, rotation/IL status, day after a doubleheader — one model, since a planned bullpen day is really the extreme end of the same short-outing spectrum as an early pull | **Built, mixed — baseline positive, v1 adds real discrimination but comes back `overconfidence_risk`.** Baseline LR beats the harder naive floor (PR-AUC 0.4199 vs. 0.3094, +0.1104) — the widest baseline margin of any model here. v1 adds a trailing-3-start IP trend and team rest days: PR-AUC 0.4402 (+0.0203 vs. baseline, +0.1308 vs. naive), ROC-AUC 0.6842. But this model predicts at start grain already — the grain a short-outing prop resolves on — so the reliability+resolution verdict runs directly (no PA→game rollup needed) and comes back `overconfidence_risk`: real added spread (resolution +0.0113) but slightly less honest than naive (reliability +0.0003), the same verdict bb_predictor's own game-grain check hit. Zero new feature engineering for the baseline; v1 generalized `game_context.py`'s `build_pitcher_start_ip_this_season` with a `window` param (trailing-N vs. season-to-date) to get the recent-form feature. Label is realized IP ≤ 4.0 only; explicit-opener detection isn't wired up yet. |

Weaker candidates considered and set aside for now: player rest-day / will-not-start (useful as a supporting feature, not a standalone target), stolen-base attempts (narrow population, smaller market), home-run props (tempting because power is skill-driven, but still has real batted-ball/weather variance in the tail — not clearly better than the hit problem that already stalled).

**Supporting infrastructure, not a standalone prop: `batters_faced_predictor`.** K-prop's total-strikeout prediction depends on `game_context.py`'s `build_expected_batters_faced` — a hand-built shrinkage cascade (pitcher's own last season → his team's → the league's, blended toward this season's emerging average) estimating how many batters a starter will face tonight. This model asks whether a properly-tuned regressor beats that formula outright. **Built, real improvement, then a clean negative.** Baseline: a tuned XGBoost (shallow trees, low learning rate, early stopping — untuned XGBoost badly overfits at this data size) beats the cascade on the cascade's own inputs, MAE 2.7411 vs. 2.8609 — the first model in this project to beat a frozen shared-infrastructure formula on real evidence. v1's new features (opposing-lineup traffic, rest days) added nothing once XGBoost was properly tuned (2.7347, noise-level). A residual error analysis (classic "look at the worst predictions" pass) then found two distinct failure modes: established starters over-predicted when pulled early despite a clean box line (in-game injury/rain-delay signature), and cold-start pitchers under-predicted. v2 targeted the first directly — trailing-3-start workload trend, the pitcher's *own* rest days (not team rest, which is ~always 1), and a pitches-thrown workload-density signal — and won: MAE 2.6471, a real, not noise-level, improvement. v3 targeted cold-start pitchers with a multi-season lookback (recovering track record for a pitcher who missed a full season to injury rather than falling straight to a team/league average) and came back flat (2.6479) — that bucket's error turned out to be genuine small-sample variance, not a missing-data gap. v4 came back flat too (2.6432 — a same-season anomaly-count feature aimed at the same established-starter-pull mode v2 partially closed, essentially unused by the model per feature importance), closing that failure-mode thread. v5 and v6 then tried a different mechanism entirely — the opposing team's own scoring, not the pitcher's own workload or history — and both also came back flat: v5 (2.6369) tried scoring level (win_pct/runs_scored/run_diff), v6 (2.6349) tried scoring volatility (mean/std/max of runs scored, capturing "can this offense explode for a blowout"), each at both a season and a trailing-5-game window. Neither cut of opposing-team scoring ranked meaningfully in feature importance. `build_expected_batters_faced` itself is still unmodified in production; a switch-over is a separate, not-yet-made decision, and v2 (2.6471) remains the strongest model after five follow-up passes (v3-v6) failed to beat it. Full numbers: `src/models/batters_faced_predictor/{baseline,v1,v2,v3,v4,v5,v6}_results.md`, `ROADMAP.md`'s 2026-08-26 entries.

**Still open: the batter-side hit-probability decomposition.** The mechanism reframe doesn't retire the original problem — `hit_predictor` still needs real batter-side signal beyond the current statistical baseline (this-season batting average shrunk toward last-season's, k=100). Five bundled ML feature sets (v1–v5) all lose to that two-line formula, and why is still unresolved (`ROADMAP.md` backlog item 2). Not abandoned — this runs alongside the pitcher-side sub-problems above, not instead of them.
