## 2026-09-05 — README rework: from "noise floor" thesis to data + prop matrix + ranked table

Reworked `README.md`'s framing after auditing whether the original "noise floor" thesis (project pivoted from `hit_predictor`'s noisy per-PA target toward lower-noise, pre-game-mechanism sub-problems) was actually validated, not just narrated. Findings from that audit: the directional claim holds better than nothing — `k_predictor` and `n_pa_predictor` both cleared naive with `real_improvement`/CI-verified wins — but it's not the clean story the original prose told. Evidence against: `bb_predictor` and `short_outing_predictor` both landed on `overconfidence_risk`, not a clean win (2 wins, 2 mixed, not a sweep); `k_predictor`'s win doesn't survive real market odds (confirmed calibration bug, no betting edge); and `hit_predictor`'s root cause for losing to a two-line statistical formula is still genuinely unresolved (`ROADMAP.md`'s hit_predictor backlog item 2 — a methodology confound, bundled features with no incremental verification, hasn't been ruled out as the real explanation, separate from "the target is just noisy").

Replaced README's narrative "thesis" + "dividing line" sections with a **Data** section (S3 sources/coverage/pipeline status), a short one-paragraph working-reason folded into the prop-matrix section (not a standalone essay), and a **Models ranked by performance vs. naive** table (rank/bin/vs-naive, ranked by verdict tier then relative lift within tier — metrics differ by model so this is directional, not literal). The fuller evidence-for/evidence-against synthesis from the audit is preserved here rather than in README, per this repo's own doc-split convention (dated rationale lives in this file, README stays lean):

**Evidence for the working thesis** (pitcher-side props are ahead because a pitcher's game total rests on more plate-appearance-equivalent draws than a batter's does — ~22 batters faced vs. ~4 PAs): `k_predictor` and `n_pa_predictor` both clear naive with verified `real_improvement`; the three-true-outcomes mechanism (K/BB/HBP resolve without contact) has independent support in the sabermetrics literature.

**Evidence against, or still open**: the mechanism-side record is 2 wins / 2 mixed, not a sweep; `k_predictor`'s win fails against real 2026 DraftKings odds (confirmed calibration bug, coin-flip disagreement win rate, short of the ~52.4% break-even bar); a within-game correlation check (`icc_check.py`, ICC≈0.01) found PA outcomes within one start are NOT fully independent, which weakens (without invalidating) the pure "averages over more independent draws" framing; and `hit_predictor`'s specific failure mode vs. a two-line statistical formula is still unattributed to noise vs. methodology.

**A separate, more concrete finding surfaced while building the prop-matrix diagram (`docs/prop_matrix.svg`)**: `hit_predictor`'s and `bb_predictor`'s existing game-grain checks (`run_pa_vs_game_grain_check`) both group by the *realized* PA count from the box score, not a predicted one — they've only ever been backtested, never built as something that could run before a game starts. `k_predictor` is the only model in the repo with a genuine pre-game rollup (predicted `batters_faced` → synthetic slot expansion → combine). This is a real prerequisite gap sitting in front of any batter-side prop reaching production, independent of whether its classifier ever beats naive. Also surfaced: `bb_predictor`'s existing "game-grain" result is actually a Batter Walks attempt (grouped by batter), not a Pitcher Walks Allowed one as the model's own framing implies — the pitcher-allowed version of both Hits and Walks has never been built at all, despite needing no new classifier (`hit_predictor`/`bb_predictor` + `batters_faced_predictor` already exist) — flagged in `docs/prop_matrix.svg` as the cheapest next lift.

## 2026-09-02

k_predictor v13: batter-grain Poisson GLM total-K model, a third grain tried on 6(c)'s finding that v6's aggregation is overconfident against real 2026 DK odds — one row per real lineup batter per start (~9/start, not v6's ~22-40 PA-grain slots or v12's single start-grain row), predicting that batter's own strikeout count against the starter with his expected plate-appearance count entered as a Poisson exposure OFFSET (fixed coefficient of 1 in log-space) rather than a plain feature, so total-start-K is the closed-form SUM of 9 independent Poisson predictions (Poisson-additivity — no new distribution-combination code needed, unlike v12's `negative_binomial_pmf`). Deliberately a GLM, not XGBoost, isolating the grain/exposure hypothesis from a model-family confound (same reasoning as v12 vs. v6; this repo's own baseline already found linear-in-log-odds models beating XGBoost on K-rate targets). New TDD'd production code: `build_team_bullpen_pa_share` (rolling manager quick-hook tendency, `game_context.py`), `build_batter_expected_pa` (collapses `build_batter_slot_expansion`'s per-slot output to one row per real batter, `game_context.py`), `poisson_pmf` (`count_distribution.py`). Full design reasoning published as a design artifact ("Batter-Grain K's", https://claude.ai/code/artifact/5f696523-9031-46db-bfbd-3e7b640bbe7a). **A real, severe pre-existing bug was found and fixed mid-build, caught via a cheap single-season diagnostic before wasting a full 8-season run on the wrong hypothesis**: `build_batter_slot_expansion` merges `batting_order` onto `(gamepk, lineup_position)` with no team-awareness, so whenever a `gamepk` has two starts (home SP + away SP, always), roughly half of all synthetic slots collide against the WRONG team's lineup (a pitcher's own teammates, never his real opponents) — match rate against the real target was 49.9% (an exact home/away-collision signature) before a fix (team-scope `batting_order` per start, expand home and away starters separately, concatenate) brought it to 99.7%. **This bug pre-dates v13 and lives in all 6 existing callers of `build_batter_slot_expansion`, including `score_2026_test_dates.py` — the exact script that produced v6's own published 13x real-odds headline number — not yet audited or fixed there.** **Result, post-fix: hypothesis rejected, worse than both v6 and v12.** v13's real-2026-odds reliability gap is 70x the market's (vs. v12's 36x, v6's 13x), and its disagreement win rate (46.7%) is the first of the three to land significantly BELOW the ~52.4% break-even bar (p=0.007), not just indistinguishable from a coin flip — no edge, actively worse than the market on this evidence. v13 looks at least as good as v12 and better than v6 on the disconnected 2024 val-season checks (predicted mean K 4.784 vs. realized 4.836, a real working point estimate unlike v12's), making this now a THREE-TIME-replicated pattern (v6's isotonic-recalibration-doesn't-transfer finding, v12's better-on-val/worse-on-real split, v13's same split, more extreme) that whatever drives real-market miscalibration is very unlikely to be explained by grain or independence-assumption choice alone. v6's tuned XGBoost remains the standing production candidate; three independent modeling approaches have now been tried against the real-odds backtest and none clears the bar for betting use. See `v13_results.md` for full detail.

## 2026-09-01

k_predictor v12: start-grain Negative Binomial (NB2) total-K model, testing a specific fix for 6(c)'s finding that v6's Poisson-binomial aggregation is significantly overconfident against real 2026 DK odds (13x the market's reliability) — the hypothesis was that treating ~22 per-slot outcomes as independent Bernoulli trials ignores real within-game correlation (same game/park/weather/bullpen), and that a single fitted NB2 dispersion parameter would absorb it. New TDD'd `negative_binomial_pmf` in `count_distribution.py` (7 tests); `statsmodels` NegativeBinomial GLM fit directly on one row per SP start, no slot expansion, no Poisson-binomial combine. Also closed a real gap in the reused start-grain feature frame (previously merged the pitcher's own team's batting K-rate instead of the actual opponent's — fixed via `build_team_batter_strikeout_rolling_feats` re-keyed onto `opp_team_id`, coefficient significant p=0.003). **Result: hypothesis rejected.** On the same 1,160-start real-odds sample, v12 is 36x more miscalibrated than the market (worse than v6's 13x), and its disagreement win rate (48.9%) is coin-flip territory, same "no edge" conclusion as v6+isotonic. The informative part: v12 looks *better* than v6 on the disconnected 2024 val-season check — the opposite of its real-market result, a second data point (after 6(c)'s isotonic-recalibration finding) that the val-season check doesn't reliably predict real-market calibration. Also caught and fixed a real preprocessing bug (`pd.get_dummies(dummy_na=True)` without `drop_first=True` made the GLM's dummy block collinear with the intercept, `NaN` std errors; point predictions were unaffected). v6's tuned XGBoost remains the standing production candidate; see the Near-term backlog's item 6(d) and `v12_results.md` for full detail.
